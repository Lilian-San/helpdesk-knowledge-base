What is an SLA?

A Service Level Agreement (SLA) is a documented contract between a service provider and a customer that defines the expected level of service. 

It outlines the responsibilities, performance standards, response times, availability targets and penalties or remedies if the agreed service levels are not achieved.

Why are SLAs important?

SLAs create clear, achievable expectations between service providers and customers in how services will be delivered, monitored and handled. 

They are also a good tool to measure quality of service and track performance to identify areas that need improvement.

Types of SLA

**Customer level SLA:** This is used for a specific customer or client, covering all services under one agreement. It is more personalised and aligned to customer needs.

**Service level SLA:** This applies to a specific service provided to multiple customers. The same standards apply to all customers making it easier to manage.

**Multilevel SLA:** This SLA combines multiple agreements to address customer and service needs. This could include customer-level agreements and service level agreements. 

## Common SLA Metrics

- First Response Time (FRT): How quickly a service desk acknowledges a ticket.  
- Resolution Time: How long it takes to completely resolve an issue.  
- System Availability (Uptime): The percentage of time a service is available.  
- Mean Time to Resolve (MTTR): The average time taken to resolve incidents.  
- Customer Satisfaction (CSAT): Feedback collected from customers after support interactions.

## Priority Levels

Many organisations use a priority matrix to determine how quickly an issue should be responded to and solved.

Priority targets will vary between organisations, the example below demonstrates a typical ITIL style priority matrix and response model.

- P1: Priority 1, acknowledge within 10 minutes, resolve within 4 hours.
- P2: Priority 2, acknowledge within 15 minutes, resolve within 8 hours.
- P3: Priority 3, acknowledge within 1 hour, resolve within 2 working days.
- P4: Priority 4, acknowledge within 4 hours, resolve within 5 working days.
- P5: Priority 5, acknowledge within one business day, resolve within 10 working days.

**Impact:** The severity of the fault, how much downtime or how many users are affected.

**Urgency:** How quickly the fault needs to be resolved.

|             |      | Urgency |     |
| ----------- | ---- | ------- | --- |
|             | High | Med     | Low |
| High Impact | P1   | P2      | P3  |
| Med Impact  | P2   | P3      | P4  |
| Low Impact  | P3   | P4      | P5  |
## Example 1:

**Issue:** Company wide email outage

**Impact :** High (all users affected)

**Urgency:** High (business operations disrupted)

**Priority:** P1

## Example 2:

**Issue:** One user is unable to access a shared mailbox

**Impact:** Low

**Urgency:** Medium

**Priority:** P4
